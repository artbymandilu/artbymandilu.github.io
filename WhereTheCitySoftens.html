Where The City Softens

This project blurs the boundary between land, water, and architecture. Partially embedded within the ground, the intervention emerges as an extension of the landscape, drawing from the geometric forms that shape the site. Repeated patterns appear as both earthworks and inhabitable spaces, allowing the constructed and natural to share a common language.

Academic Term: 2026 Fall

Instructors: Jeremy Ficca and Maryam Karimi

Project Description

Winding paths weave through these forms, guiding visitors toward gathering spaces carved into the land and docking areas set within the water. Together, these spaces dissolve traditional distinctions between ground and architecture, creating a landscape that is both built and grown.

Approaching the venue, guests first see the building's sloping roofs, which frame views toward the city and draw visitors deeper into the site. Arriving at the theater, the corrugated structure reveals itself as both building and landscape. Large garage-style doors open the performance space to the outdoors, allowing the theater to transform from an enclosed venue into an open stage framed by the river and skyline beyond.

Continuing downward along the site’s natural topography or its ADA-accessible pathways, visitors gradually descend beneath gently terraced seating embedded within the land itself. The architecture becomes increasingly inseparable from the landscape, with earthen ramps and pathways guiding movement through the terrain rather than across it. At the water’s edge, the journey culminates in a floating barge stage, extending the performance space onto the river and reinforcing the project’s connection between city, land, and water.
